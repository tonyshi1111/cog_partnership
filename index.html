01 · The wedge

Cognition owns a quadrant no one else can reach.

The marketing copy for every AI coding agent rhymes. Cursor, Claude Code, Replit, Manus, Cognition — all promise an "autonomous teammate." Once you look at who's paying, for what job, in what environment, the products diverge sharply. The partnership strategy that follows assumes we own one quadrant of that market — and that owning it means doubling down on the partner motion that put us there.

Thesis
Our wedge isn't "autonomous coding" — that's table stakes. It's async work, sold to enterprises, distributed through services channels. Three things compounding. None replicable inside six months by a model-layer competitor. The partnership strategy is how we lengthen that lead.

The Windsurf acquisition is the proof point. We didn't just buy technology — we bought distribution, ARR, and the right to live in the engineer's IDE alongside the engineer's Jira board. That's a bundle no competitor can replicate without the same kind of move, and Cursor has neither the capital position nor the enterprise muscle to execute it.

Sequencing matters more than selection

SIs first — they bring volume and validate enterprise-grade. Then hyperscalers (procurement velocity, global reach). Then workflow systems of record (sticky integration that locks in the SI deals). Then regulated verticals (the moat play). Done in a different order — say, chasing verticals before SI volume — burns capital faster.
